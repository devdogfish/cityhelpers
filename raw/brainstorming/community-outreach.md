# Community outreach

The meeting proposed sponsoring senior socials, coffee/tea gatherings, service giveaways, and door hangers. A participant described a neighbour gaining car-detailing customers after a building giveaway; this is an anecdote, not evidence of City Helpers results.

The hypothesis is that meeting a representative and seeing a useful service can make City Helpers familiar and easier to trust. Begin with an existing gathering through a willing community partner. Demonstrate one task, explain the dispatch fee and separate labour charge, and give attendees a simple next step. Hosting a standalone event adds cost and coordination; compare that option only after learning from a small collaboration.

Match the offer to the setting. Seniors in apartments may need lifting, organizing, or small indoor jobs; yard-work messaging belongs with people responsible for their own outdoor space. Door hangers are a proposed awareness tool, with distribution arranged through the building or organizer. For a flyer or door hanger, lead with one relevant job, explain the charges, and provide one booking or enquiry route. Suggested wording developed for these notes: “Heavy furniture to move? Get a helping hand.” Use the same concrete offer when introducing the service at an event. A giveaway needs a defined task, labour allowance, worker availability, and funding; a free dispatch alone does not pay for the work.

**Suggested first implementation:** one partner, one event, one service offer, and a trackable contact route. Record event cost and staff time, qualified enquiries, paid dispatches after the event, completed jobs, and attendee questions. Do not count the free demonstration as acquired paid business. Decide whether to repeat based on both demand and fulfilment. Budget and partner access remain unknown.
