We have two objectives: learn how people become customers and increase sustainable paid business. A test can produce useful learning without producing enough customers; report both outcomes separately. This is a proposed measurement framework, not a description of existing analytics.

| Stage | Record | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Channel, campaign/partner code, date, city | Where interest originates. |
| Enquiry | Unique enquiries, task, service area, booking for self/another person when relevant | Whether the audience and jobs fit. |
| Booking | Unique visitors, booking starts, platform payments, submitted requests | Where people stop. |
| Delivery | Matches, completed jobs, cancellations, no-shows, refunds | Whether acquisition creates delivered value. |
| Return | Repeat paid bookings and time to next booking | Whether relationships continue. |

**Core calculations:** paid conversion = paying customers / eligible visitors for the same period and source; acquisition cost = attributable acquisition spending / new paying customers; completion rate = completed jobs / submitted requests. Include event costs, incentives, and staff effort when comparing channels. Record dispatch volume separately from unique customers. Worker wages paid directly are not City Helpers revenue.

Use distinct links or referral codes and an optional “How did you hear about us?” field for offline activity. Link enquiry, payment, request, and outcome records without publishing personal details. Avoid treating ad clicks, event attendance, and website sessions as interchangeable counts.

Before each test, agree a budget cap, evaluation date, outcome target, and operational limits. Numeric targets require current costs and capacity; none are established yet. Low-volume tests can reveal usability problems and customer language without supporting precise conversion forecasts. See [open questions](open-questions.html).
