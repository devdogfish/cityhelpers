**Customer and everyday need.** City Helpers’ primary audience is seniors aged 65+ living independently in Toronto/GTA, booking practical household help themselves. One working profile is an older homeowner, living alone or with a partner, who manages their home but struggles with particular physical tasks: mowing, hedge trimming, leaf cleanup, snow shovelling, lifting, garage cleaning, or small household jobs. The CEO’s project transcript centres people wanting to remain at home and describes his own difficulty finding help after a stroke. Independence, maintaining the home, and affordable assistance are the core motivations; household composition and purchasing habits remain hypotheses, not verified customer data.

**Location and purchasing capacity.** Erick’s [pre-meeting audience assumptions](https://devdogfish.github.io/cityhelpers/raw/source-material/erick-pre-meeting-ideas.txt) distinguish urban seniors in condos, private independent or assisted living, and family homes from seniors maintaining full households farther out in the GTA. These are his hypotheses, not measured customer data. He also notes tenant-to-tenant service advertising in condos: an existing alternative to understand. Match indoor help to residents’ unmet needs and yard work to households responsible for outdoor maintenance; confirm who books and pays in shared or assisted settings. Toronto city had 476,990 residents aged 65+ in 2021, representing 17.1% of its population. The City identifies senior concentrations around Bridle Path–Sunnybrook–York Mills, Rosedale–Moore Park, Casa Loma, and northern Scarborough. These are neighbourhoods to investigate, with final targeting dependent on suitable homes and available helpers—not proven customer hotspots. The commercial target can afford labour plus the dispatch fee; homeownership alone does not establish disposable income. No exact income, gender, or retirement-status filter is supported. [Source: City of Toronto, 2021 Census backgrounder](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/9654-City-Planning-2021-Census-Backgrounder-Age-Sex-Gender-DwellingType.pdf).

**Digital platforms and habits.** Seniors are reachable online: in 2022, 85.4% of Ontario residents aged 65+ used the internet. Nationally, 74.7% of seniors used email, 50.9% instant messaging, 43.5% social networks, and 40.7% booked appointments online. A 2025 Canadian online-panel survey found Facebook the most popular social platform among respondents over 60, with YouTube and Instagram also used. Facebook, Messenger, and email therefore merit initial attention; neighbourhood Facebook groups and Google searches for specific chores are acquisition hypotheses to test. These population findings do not establish GTA customers’ platform preferences or willingness to book City Helpers. [Statistics Canada, 2022 data](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/o1/en/plus/4288-canadian-seniors-more-connected-ever); [The Dais, 2025 survey](https://dais.ca/reports/survey-of-online-harms-in-canada-2025/).

**Buying behaviour and trust.** Likely triggers are seasonal chores, reduced ability to complete a task, or the loss of a regular helper. Test whether customers seek neighbour recommendations, compare providers, and rebook after a satisfactory job. Expected purchase questions: Who is coming? What does vetting mean? What will the whole job cost? When will it happen? What if nobody comes? Explain the brief’s $9.99 dispatch fee separately from labour, show credible local testimonials, and offer simple booking with a visible human contact. Community maintenance services are existing alternatives, sometimes subsidized. Lead with “Get help with the chores you want done”; family-assisted booking is a secondary path. Behavioural claims here require validation. [Toronto’s existing home-support options](https://www.toronto.ca/community-people/children-parenting/seniors-services/seniors-health-services/).
