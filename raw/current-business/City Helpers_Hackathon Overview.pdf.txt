CITY HELPERS
Digital Marketing Hackathon — One-Week Deep-Dive
Project
Project Objective

City Helpers is looking for a practical digital marketing strategy to help generate real customers
and dispatch orders in the Toronto/GTA market.

The current business model allows a customer to pay a $9.99 dispatch fee to submit a request
for help. City Helpers then connects the customer with potential local helpers.

The goal of this project is not to create a broad marketing plan covering everything.

Instead, we want the team to conduct a deep dive into the areas that are most likely to
determine whether City Helpers can acquire customers efficiently and scale.

The four key questions are:

   1.​ Who should City Helpers target first?
   2.​ Where can we reach those customers?
   3.​ What should we say to get them to take action?
   4.​ How do we turn website visitors into paying dispatch customers?

PRIORITY 3 — The City Helpers Marketing Message

Objective
Determine what City Helpers should actually say to potential customers.

The team should investigate how competitors and alternative services describe themselves.

Then develop several possible City Helpers positioning statements.

For example:

Problem-focused

Have a job you can't do yourself? Find local help through City Helpers.

Convenience-focused

Need an extra pair of hands? City Helpers connects you with local helpers for short-term
jobs.
Business-focused

Need extra help for a short-term project? Find local workers through City Helpers.

These are only examples.

The students should conduct research and develop their own alternatives.

Test different approaches

The team should create sample:

   ●​   Google advertisements
   ●​   Facebook/Instagram advertisements
   ●​   Business advertisements
   ●​   Social-media posts
   ●​   Short video concepts

Each should answer:

Who is this for?

What problem does it solve?

Why City Helpers?

What should the customer do next?



PRIORITY 4 — Website Conversion
Objective
This is one of the most important parts of the project.

A marketing campaign is not useful if it sends people to a website and they don't understand
what to do.

The students should conduct a customer journey audit of CityHelpers.ca.

Imagine that you have never heard of City Helpers.

Within the first few seconds, can you understand:

   ●​ What City Helpers is?
   ●​   Who it is for?
   ●​   What types of jobs can be requested?
   ●​   How much does it cost?
   ●​   How does the process work?
   ●​   What happens after I submit a request?
   ●​   Who performs the work?
   ●​   Why should I trust the service?
   ●​   What do I do next?

Students should identify:

Anything that could prevent a visitor from becoming a $9.99 dispatch customer.

Then recommend specific changes.

For example:

   ●​   Homepage messaging
   ●​   Calls to action
   ●​   Request-help button
   ●​   Explanation of the $9.99 dispatch
   ●​   How-it-works section
   ●​   Trust elements
   ●​   Customer testimonials
   ●​   Service categories
   ●​   Landing pages
   ●​   Mobile experience
   ●​   Checkout/request process

The students should prioritize their recommendations as:

HIGH PRIORITY

Changes that could directly affect customer conversion.

MEDIUM PRIORITY

Changes that could improve the customer experience.

LOW PRIORITY

Nice-to-have improvements that can wait.

THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE
Don't tell us what you think we want to hear.

We want your research and your reasoning.

If you believe a particular advertising channel could work, explain why.

If you are uncertain, explain what experiment could determine whether it works.

If you think something City Helpers is currently doing should change, explain why.
The objective isn't to produce a beautiful marketing report.

The objective is to give City Helpers a practical customer-acquisition strategy that can
actually be tested after the hackathon.
