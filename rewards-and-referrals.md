Erick proposed **CityHelpers Rewards (CHR)** and **CityHelpers Partners (CHP)** before team meeting 1. His [original message](https://devdogfish.github.io/cityhelpers/raw/source-material/erick-pre-meeting-ideas.txt) supplies the programme details referenced in the meeting. The aim is to encourage customers and workers to introduce new customers and keep using the service.

## Erick’s proposed programmes

**CHR — customer rewards.** Individuals and businesses earn points for referring new subscribers and using services. Points unlock perks such as free services. Participating workers deliver those services and bill City Helpers “at cost,” with City Helpers funding the reward.

**CHP — worker participation.** Workers commit a defined number of services at cost, invoiced directly to City Helpers. They also earn points for completed services, referred clients, and customer feedback, with awards or recognition. Erick calls the feedback measure NPS; recommendation scores and job satisfaction ratings should be defined separately before choosing the measure.

Erick also suggests points for public reviews and social posts using designated hashtags. Keep these as separate promotional options requiring platform-policy review before inclusion. Customer feedback can be requested after every completed job without making a positive review a condition of a reward.

## Practical implementation recommendation

Start with one referral benefit before building a points system: issue a capped credit after a referred customer’s first paid, completed job. This adapts Erick’s signup reward to the business objective of gaining paying customers. Use a referral code or link, one reward per new customer, and a simple record connecting referrer, booking, issued credit, and redemption. Show the credit value, expiry, eligibility, and whether it covers dispatch or labour before signup.

For service rewards, define an eligible task, maximum labour allowance, service area, and monthly redemption cap. Agree worker compensation in advance: “at cost” needs an explicit amount and scope. City Helpers should confirm a participating worker before promising the appointment, pay the agreed invoice, and explain any customer extras. Recognize workers for reliable completed jobs and customer feedback using published criteria.

Before launch, City Helpers would set the reward budget from contribution per completed job and assign someone to handle credits, invoices, and disputes. Evaluate referred paying customers, completed jobs, repeat bookings, and total reward cost; signup and point totals alone do not demonstrate growth. These are recommendations for implementation after the hackathon.
