City Helpers wants to attract paying customers in Toronto/GTA and learn how to do so at a sustainable cost. Its service connects people who need help with local workers for small jobs. The CEO describes the household audience as “people who want to stay in their home”. Our initial focus is seniors arranging help themselves with chores such as yard work, cleaning, and lifting. Other homeowners and businesses remain part of the broader brief.

The marketing challenge is to help those customers discover City Helpers, understand the service, and feel confident enough to pay for a dispatch. The CEO believes awareness and explanation need work, particularly on the website: “a marketing campaign is not useful if it sends people to a website that they don't understand.” We therefore need to examine both how people find the service and what happens when they arrive. We do not yet have customer data showing whether awareness, trust, pricing, or difficulty booking is the biggest barrier.

They want us to recommend which customers to pursue first, where to reach them, what to say, and how to make requesting help easier. These decisions should form a practical 90-day test plan, with sample messages, specific website improvements, and estimates of spending, website visits, paid dispatches, and customer acquisition cost. Each estimate needs a clear assumption behind it so the business can test the recommendation and judge the results. The immediate deliverable is a focused plan the team can put into practice after the hackathon.

Success means generating paid dispatches at a cost the business can sustain, with completed jobs and repeat bookings helping us assess lasting value. The brief describes a $9.99 dispatch fee, with worker labour paid separately. The marketing budget, current conversion rate, repeat usage, and acceptable acquisition cost are still unknown, so our recommendations must make those assumptions explicit. Toronto/GTA is the first test market; what works there can inform expansion into other cities.
