# Customer research

**Status: proposed research plan.** The team asked how to understand customer behaviour and suggested event signups, surveys, customer profiles, testimonials, and platform data. The aim is to learn which jobs people need, how they find help, and what prevents booking.

Start with existing customers, relevant seniors who have not used City Helpers, and people who started but did not complete a request if the business can reach them. Keep their experiences separate. Ask about the last real task they needed help with: what they did, whom they contacted, what it cost, why they chose that option, and what went wrong. Ask whether they booked for themselves or someone else without assuming either answer.

At events, use a short optional enquiry or feedback form. During booking, collect service requirements; add research questions only where they do not obstruct the task. Use testimonials as clues to motivations, recognizing that they are selected positive experiences. The meeting mentioned scraping or a Meta API, but no access or useful data fields were established; first inspect aggregate reporting available in the business's own accounts. Public platform activity does not reveal private preferences or purchase intent.

**Smallest test:** a handful of interviews plus an observed booking walkthrough. Record anonymized observations, direct customer wording, contradictions, and questions that remain open. Treat the sample as qualitative learning, not a representative survey. Compare findings with actual bookings when available.
