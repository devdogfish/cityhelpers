City Helpers wants more paying customers in Toronto/GTA. Our initial focus is seniors booking household help, reflecting the CEO’s aim to serve “people who want to stay in their home”. Our working interpretation separates two related problems: understanding customer acquisition and improving it.

**1. Observability and audience knowledge.** Establish who needs the service, who actually books, where customers come from, and where they abandon the booking process. Track visits, requests, paid dispatches, and acquisition costs by channel. Success here means enough reliable evidence to identify promising audiences and diagnose barriers. We currently lack access to these figures; that does not establish whether City Helpers already collects them.

**2. Customer acquisition.** Use suitable channels, clear messaging, and easier booking to generate more paying customers at a sustainable cost. The CEO stresses that “a marketing campaign is not useful if it sends people to a website that they don't understand.” Success here means additional paid dispatches. Better measurement supports that outcome, but does not itself deliver customers.

The written hackathon brief requests a 90-day test plan covering audience, channels, messages, website improvements, and estimated results. Our recommendations should specify both what each test will teach us and how it could generate customers, with budgets, assumptions, and success measures for each. The CEO’s transcript supports testing and acquisition work; the explicit 90-day timeframe comes from the brief.
